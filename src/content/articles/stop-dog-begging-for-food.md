Begging survives because it's intermittently rewarded — even if it "works" only one time out of twenty, that occasional payoff is often enough to keep the behavior going strong. Fixing it requires consistency across the whole household, not just willpower at one meal.

## Why Begging Is So Persistent

Behaviors reinforced on an unpredictable schedule (a dropped piece of food here, a soft-hearted family member there) are actually more resistant to fading than behaviors rewarded every time. That's why one inconsistent household member can undo weeks of everyone else's discipline.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Get full household agreement.** Begging only fades if literally no one feeds from the table — one exception keeps the behavior alive.
2. **Give your dog a job during meals.** Teach a "place" or mat cue, rewarding your dog for settling on their bed during your meal instead of hovering nearby.
3. **Reinforce calm distance, not proximity.** Toss a reward to your dog's mat periodically during the meal, specifically for staying there — this actively reinforces the alternative behavior rather than just ignoring begging.
4. **Manage before you train.** If begging is deeply ingrained, use a gate or crate during meals for a few weeks while the "place" cue gets solid, then fade back to relying on the cue alone.
5. **Keep feeding your dog on a normal schedule.** Begging isn't about hunger for most well-fed dogs — it's a learned response to food-adjacent situations, so restricting meals isn't the fix.

## Common Mistakes

- One household member "just this once" feeding from the table, resetting progress for everyone else.
- Yelling or pushing a begging dog away, which doesn't teach an alternative behavior — it just adds unpredictability.
- Expecting an immediate fix; intermittent reinforcement histories take longer to fade than consistent ones.

## Setting Realistic Expectations

Because begging is often maintained by occasional "wins," expect a few weeks of full consistency before you see a real drop-off. If your household struggles with consistency, a structured training program with clear household rules can make the plan easier to stick to.
