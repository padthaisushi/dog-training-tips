Chewing itself is normal dog behavior — it's a natural stress reliever, jaw exerciser, and way to explore the world. The goal isn't to eliminate chewing, but to redirect it firmly onto appropriate items.

## Common Causes

- **Teething** (puppies, roughly 3–6 months): chewing relieves gum discomfort.
- **Boredom or excess energy:** furniture becomes an outlet when there's nothing better to do.
- **Anxiety:** destructive chewing focused on exits or personal items can overlap with separation anxiety.
- **Simple habit:** if furniture-chewing has worked (relieved boredom, gotten attention) it can become a default behavior.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Puppy-proof the environment.** Block access to tempting furniture when unsupervised, using baby gates, crates, or closed doors — management prevents rehearsal of the habit while training catches up.
2. **Provide better options, generously.** Offer a variety of chew toys and rotate them to keep interest high; a bored dog with only one old toy will find furniture more interesting.
3. **Redirect immediately, calmly.** If you catch your dog chewing furniture, calmly interrupt and offer an appropriate chew toy instead — then praise when they take it.
4. **Increase overall exercise and enrichment.** Daily walks, puzzle feeders, and training sessions burn mental and physical energy that would otherwise go toward destructive outlets.
5. **Use taste deterrents as a supplement, not a fix.** Bitter sprays can help protect specific items, but they work best alongside — not instead of — management and redirection.

## Common Mistakes

- Punishing after the fact, which doesn't connect to the chewing itself and just adds unpredictability from your dog's perspective.
- Giving old shoes or socks as chew toys, which can teach your dog that all shoes and fabric items are fair game.
- Removing all supervision too early, before appropriate chewing habits are well established.

## When to Look Deeper

If furniture chewing is concentrated around doors and windows and happens specifically when your dog is left alone, it may be separation anxiety rather than simple boredom chewing — worth addressing as its own issue. For persistent destructive chewing despite consistent management, a structured training program can help build a complete plan.
