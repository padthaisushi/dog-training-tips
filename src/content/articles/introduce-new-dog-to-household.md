First impressions matter — a rushed or chaotic introduction can set a rocky tone that takes months to undo, while a calm, structured one can lead to a smooth transition in days. The key is controlling pace and environment, not hoping everyone "just figures it out."

## Before the Introduction

- **Choose neutral territory** for the first meeting between dogs — not your resident dog's yard or home, where territorial instincts run highest.
- **Have two handlers**, one per dog, so each can be managed independently if needed.
- **Prep separate resources** in advance: separate food bowls, beds, and toys to reduce competition in the first weeks.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **First meeting on neutral ground.** Walk both dogs in parallel at a distance, gradually closing the gap only if both stay relaxed, then allow a brief, loose-leash greeting.
2. **Bring them home separately at first.** Let the new dog explore the home without the resident dog present initially, then do a calm, leashed introduction indoors.
3. **Feed and rest separately for the first week or two.** Separate bowls, separate crates or resting areas — this reduces the two biggest triggers for early conflict: food and space.
4. **Supervise all interactions closely at first.** Keep initial sessions short and positive, ending before either dog shows stress or overexcitement.
5. **Introduce cats with extra caution.** Keep the new dog leashed and calm during first cat introductions, and always give the cat an escape route and elevated safe space.

## Common Mistakes

- Letting dogs "work it out" off-leash in an uncontrolled first meeting.
- Skipping the neutral-territory step and going straight to the resident dog's home turf.
- Removing supervision too soon, before a consistent pattern of calm interaction is established.

## Realistic Timeline

Many dogs settle into a comfortable routine within 2–4 weeks, though full integration (sharing space unsupervised) can take longer depending on temperament. If you're seeing consistent tension, resource guarding, or one dog avoiding the other entirely after a few weeks, a structured, step-by-step approach can help troubleshoot the specific friction points.
