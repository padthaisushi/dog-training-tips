Clicker training uses a small, distinct sound to mark the exact moment your dog does something right — solving the timing problem that trips up a lot of beginner training. It's simple to start and works for puppies and adult dogs alike.

## Why a Clicker Works

Verbal praise varies in tone and timing every time you say it, which makes it a noisy signal. A clicker is always the same sound, delivered at the exact instant of the correct behavior, which makes it much easier for your dog to understand precisely what earned the reward.

## Step-by-Step: Getting Started

1. **Charge the clicker.** Click once, then immediately give a treat. Repeat 10–15 times in a row with no expectations — you're just teaching your dog that click = reward is coming.
2. **Test the association.** Click when your dog isn't paying attention. If they turn toward you expecting a treat, the clicker is "charged" and ready to use for real training.
3. **Mark a simple behavior.** Choose something easy, like a sit. The instant your dog's rear touches the ground, click — then treat.
4. **Add the cue word later.** Once your dog offers the behavior reliably for the click, start saying "sit" just before they do it, so the word becomes the trigger.
5. **Fade the clicker gradually.** Over time, you can move to intermittent rewards and phase out the clicker for well-established behaviors, using it mainly for teaching new ones.

## Common Mistakes

- Clicking too late, after the behavior has already ended.
- Forgetting to follow every click with a reward — a click that's not "paid" loses its meaning fast.
- Using the clicker to get attention or as a general noise-maker outside of training.

## Why It's Worth Learning

Clicker training tends to produce faster, more precise learning than praise alone because of the timing precision. If you want a structured curriculum that builds from basic clicker mechanics into full obedience and behavior work, a step-by-step training program can save a lot of trial and error.
