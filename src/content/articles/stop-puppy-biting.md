Puppy biting is one of the most common — and most frustrating — problems new owners face. The good news: it's almost always a normal developmental phase, not a sign of aggression, and it responds well to consistent training.

## Why Puppies Bite

Puppies explore the world with their mouths, and biting is also how they play with littermates. Teething (roughly 3–6 months) makes it worse, since chewing relieves gum discomfort. The goal isn't to eliminate mouthing overnight — it's to teach bite inhibition (a soft mouth) and then phase out mouthing on people entirely.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Yelp and withdraw attention.** When your puppy bites too hard, give a sharp "ow!" and stop playing for 10–15 seconds. This mimics how littermates teach bite pressure.
2. **Redirect to a toy.** The instant your puppy's mouth touches skin, calmly offer a chew toy instead. Consistency here matters more than any single correction.
3. **Reward calm mouths.** Praise and treat your puppy for licking or simply not biting during petting sessions.
4. **Manage arousal levels.** Overtired or overstimulated puppies bite more. Build in nap breaks and calm-down periods between play sessions.
5. **Never use physical punishment.** Hitting, nose-tapping, or scruffing increases fear and can worsen biting or create other behavior issues.

## Common Mistakes

- Playing rough with hands instead of toys, which teaches puppies that hands are for biting.
- Being inconsistent — letting the behavior slide when you're tired or busy.
- Expecting results in a day or two. Bite inhibition training typically takes several weeks of consistent repetition.

## When to Get Extra Help

If biting is paired with growling, stiff body language, or seems to escalate rather than improve after a few weeks of consistent training, it's worth following a structured, professional training program rather than troubleshooting alone.
