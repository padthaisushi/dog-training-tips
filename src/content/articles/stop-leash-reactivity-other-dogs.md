Leash reactivity — barking, lunging, or intense pulling toward other dogs — is usually driven by frustration, fear, or over-arousal, not aggression in the way most people picture it. The fix is a controlled, gradual process, not a single correction.

## Understand the "Threshold" Concept

Every reactive dog has a distance from a trigger (another dog) at which they can stay calm — their threshold. Training only works below that threshold; once a dog is already barking and lunging, they're too aroused to learn anything new in that moment.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Find your dog's calm distance.** Start far enough from other dogs that yours notices them but stays relaxed enough to take treats and respond to you.
2. **Pair sightings with rewards.** Every time your dog notices another dog at a comfortable distance, mark and reward — building an association between "other dog appears" and "good things happen," a method often called counter-conditioning.
3. **Decrease distance gradually, over many sessions.** Only close the gap when your dog is consistently calm at the current distance — rushing this step is the most common reason progress stalls.
4. **Have an exit plan for surprise encounters.** Practice a quick U-turn or moving behind a visual barrier (a parked car, a corner) to increase distance immediately if you're caught off guard.
5. **Manage the environment while training progresses.** Choose quieter walking routes and times, and don't feel obligated to let strange dogs approach — protecting your dog's space is part of the plan, not a failure.

## Common Mistakes

- Forcing greetings to "get it over with," which almost always backfires and increases reactivity.
- Staying at a distance that's already over threshold, where no real learning can happen.
- Punishing the reaction, which can suppress the warning signs without resolving the underlying frustration or fear — and can sometimes make dogs less predictable.

## Realistic Expectations

Leash reactivity often improves steadily over weeks to months of consistent, well-paced work — it's rarely a quick fix, but it is very trainable. For dogs with strong, established reactivity, a structured, step-by-step training program can provide the kind of precisely paced plan that's hard to replicate from general tips alone.
