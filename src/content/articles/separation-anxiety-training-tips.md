Separation anxiety is different from simple boredom-driven mischief. It shows up as panic — destructive scratching near exits, non-stop barking or howling, pacing, or accidents that only happen when the dog is alone — and it needs a different training approach.

## Confirm It's Actually Separation Anxiety

Set up a camera and leave for a short trip. True separation anxiety usually starts within minutes of your departure and centers on exit points (doors, windows). If the behavior looks more like general destructiveness throughout the day, boredom or lack of exercise may be the bigger factor.

## Step-by-Step Desensitization Plan

1. **Lower the stakes of leaving.** Practice "fake" departure cues (grabbing keys, putting on shoes) without actually leaving, so these cues stop predicting distress.
2. **Start with seconds, not minutes.** Leave the room or home for a few seconds, then return before any anxious behavior starts. Success means staying under your dog's anxiety threshold.
3. **Increase duration gradually.** Extend absences in small increments over days and weeks, always returning calmly and without a big greeting that spikes excitement.
4. **Build a "safe alone" routine.** A consistent spot, a long-lasting food puzzle, and calm background noise can help — but only as a supplement to the desensitization work, not a replacement for it.
5. **Avoid punishment entirely.** Anxiety-driven behavior isn't defiance. Punishment adds stress and typically makes separation anxiety worse.

## Common Mistakes

- Jumping to long absences (a full workday) before your dog can handle short ones calmly.
- Making departures and arrivals emotional, which raises overall arousal around the cue of you leaving.
- Assuming it will resolve on its own — untreated separation anxiety often gets worse over time.

## When to Seek Structured Help

Moderate to severe separation anxiety often benefits from a structured, step-by-step training program (and in some cases a veterinary consult), since the desensitization schedule needs to be precisely paced to the individual dog.
