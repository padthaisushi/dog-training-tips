There's no single answer, but there are realistic ranges — and knowing them helps you set expectations instead of giving up too early or assuming something's wrong.

## Rough Timelines by Goal

- **Basic cues (sit, down, stay, come):** A dog can often learn the mechanics in a few sessions over 1–2 weeks, but reliability under distraction typically takes 4–8 weeks of regular practice.
- **House training:** Most puppies show solid progress in 4–6 weeks, with full reliability often by 6 months, depending on breed size and consistency.
- **Leash manners:** Noticeable improvement within 2–3 weeks of consistent stop-and-go practice; a fully loose leash in varied environments can take a couple of months.
- **Behavior problems (reactivity, separation anxiety):** These typically take longer — often 2–6 months of gradual desensitization, since you're changing an emotional response, not just a habit.

## What Actually Determines Speed

1. **Session frequency, not just duration.** Five minutes daily beats one hour once a week — repetition spaced over time builds stronger habits than cramming.
2. **Consistency across the household.** Mixed cues or rules from different family members slow every timeline down.
3. **The dog's individual history and temperament.** A rescue with an unknown background or a naturally anxious dog may need a slower pace than a confident puppy.
4. **Whether you're following a structured plan.** Random tips applied inconsistently take far longer than a clear, sequenced curriculum.

## Setting Realistic Milestones

Instead of asking "is my dog trained yet," track specific, measurable wins: a 30-second stay, a loose leash for one block, a calm greeting at the door. Small, stacked wins are a better progress indicator than an arbitrary finish line.

If you want a clear week-by-week structure instead of guessing at timelines, a structured training program can lay out exactly what to work on at each stage.
