A good puppy class can jumpstart socialization and manners; a bad one can set you back. The format matters less than the methods and the instructor's approach — here's what to actually check.

## What to Look For

1. **Reward-based methods.** Look for classes built around positive reinforcement rather than compulsion tools (choke chains, prong collars, forced corrections) as the default teaching method.
2. **Small class sizes.** Puppies learn better — and get more individual coaching — in groups of 6–8 or fewer, not crowded classes where instructors can't give real feedback.
3. **Vaccination and health requirements.** A responsible facility requires proof of at least first-round vaccinations and a healthy bill before puppies mix, which protects your dog from exposure.
4. **A mix of training and play.** The best puppy classes balance structured skill-building (name response, sit, loose-leash basics) with supervised, appropriately matched play for socialization.
5. **Clear, credentialed instruction.** Look for trainers with recognized certifications or a clear, explainable methodology — and ask to observe a class before enrolling if possible.

## Red Flags to Avoid

- Instructors who rely primarily on punishment or intimidation to control the group.
- No screening of dogs for temperament before mixing puppies of very different sizes or play styles.
- Vague answers when you ask about their training philosophy or credentials.
- A curriculum that's identical regardless of the individual puppies' needs.

## In-Person vs. Online Options

In-person classes offer real-time feedback and structured socialization opportunities that are hard to replicate remotely. Online, self-paced training programs can be a strong complement (or alternative if local classes aren't available or affordable) — especially for building consistency between sessions or when you want a structured curriculum you can revisit anytime.

## Making the Most of Any Class

Whichever format you choose, practice the skills daily between sessions — a once-a-week class alone won't build lasting habits. Consistency at home is what actually determines whether the training sticks.
