Leash pulling happens because it works — pulling gets your dog where they want to go faster. Fixing it means changing that math so a loose leash is what actually gets them there.

## Step-by-Step Techniques

1. **The stop-and-go method.** The moment the leash goes tight, stop walking completely. Wait for slack (your dog turning back or the leash loosening), then continue. This teaches that pulling makes the walk stop, not go.
2. **Change direction instead.** As an alternative, turn and walk the other way whenever your dog pulls ahead. This keeps them checking in on your position instead of forging forward on autopilot.
3. **Reward position, not just behavior.** Mark and treat whenever your dog is naturally near your side, even mid-walk, so a loose leash gets reinforced constantly — not just during formal training.
4. **Use pattern games at the start of the walk.** A few minutes of simple back-and-forth or "find it" games before heading out burns excess energy, which reduces pulling from the first step.
5. **Choose the right equipment.** A front-clip harness reduces pulling leverage significantly compared to a back-clip harness or flat collar, though it isn't a substitute for training.

## Common Mistakes

- Continuing to walk while the leash is tight — this is the single biggest reason pulling doesn't improve.
- Only practicing in low-distraction environments and expecting it to transfer instantly to a busy street.
- Using aversive corrections that suppress pulling without changing the underlying excitement or lack of leash skills.

## Building a Loose-Leash Habit

Expect short, frequent training walks (10–15 minutes, several times a week) to progress faster than occasional long walks where old habits get rehearsed the whole way. For dogs with strong pulling habits already ingrained, a structured leash-training program can give you a clear week-by-week plan instead of trial and error.
