A reliable recall can be the single most important cue you teach — it keeps your dog safe around traffic, wildlife, and unpredictable situations. It's also one of the most commonly rushed cues, which is why so many dogs "know" it at home but ignore it outside.

## Why Recall Training Often Fails

Most weak recalls come from one root cause: the cue got used mostly in situations where coming back was the least fun option (ending playtime at the park, going in for a bath). If "come" predicts something your dog doesn't want, they'll learn to tune it out.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Start with a fresh cue word.** If "come" is already worn out, pick a new word or use your dog's name plus a clear cue like "here."
2. **Build value in low-distraction settings first.** Call your dog from a few feet away at home, and reward generously — high-value treats, not just kibble.
3. **Make coming back always pay off.** Even if you're calling your dog in from play, reward and then release them to keep playing sometimes, so recall doesn't always mean "fun is over."
4. **Add distance and distractions gradually.** Practice in the yard, then a quiet park, then busier areas — always on a long line until reliability is proven.
5. **Use a long line, not off-leash, during training.** A 15–30 foot training lead lets you enforce the cue without chasing your dog if they ignore it.

## Common Mistakes

- Calling a dog to end something they enjoy every single time.
- Repeating the cue over and over when the dog doesn't respond, which teaches them the first (or fifth) call doesn't matter.
- Punishing a dog after they finally come back — even a late recall should be rewarded, not scolded.

## Realistic Expectations

A dependable recall in low-distraction settings often takes 2–4 weeks; full reliability around high-distraction environments (other dogs, wildlife) can take months of consistent practice. If your dog has a history of bolting or ignoring recall entirely, a structured training program can help rebuild the cue from scratch with a clear progression.
