Name recognition is arguably the most important early skill — nearly every other cue depends on your puppy first orienting to you when you say their name. It's also one of the fastest things to teach, often taking just a few days of short sessions.

## Step-by-Step Method

1. **Say the name once, then reward any glance.** In a quiet room, say your puppy's name in a happy tone. The moment they look toward you — even briefly — mark ("yes!") and reward immediately.
2. **Keep sessions short and frequent.** Five repetitions, several times a day, works better than one long drilling session.
3. **Avoid repeating the name if there's no response yet.** Say it once, wait a beat, and if there's no response, simply try again a bit later rather than repeating it three times in a row — you don't want the name to lose meaning through overuse.
4. **Increase distraction gradually.** Once your puppy responds reliably at home, practice in the yard, then on walks, rewarding every response generously in each new environment.
5. **Never pair the name with something negative.** Don't use your puppy's name right before a correction or something unpleasant (like nail trims) — this can create hesitation to respond over time.

## Common Mistakes

- Overusing the name as background chatter, which dilutes its meaning as a specific cue.
- Calling the name repeatedly without reward when the puppy doesn't respond, teaching that ignoring it has no real consequence.
- Only practicing indoors, then expecting instant reliability in a distracting park.

## Why This Skill Matters So Much

A strong name response is the foundation for recall, redirecting attention away from something concerning, and simply keeping your puppy checked in with you in new environments. If you want name recognition built into a broader, structured early-training curriculum instead of taught in isolation, a step-by-step puppy training program can help tie it together with everything else.
