Chasing cars, bikes, or joggers is typically driven by predatory instinct triggered by fast movement — it's less about aggression and more about an automatic chase response. Because it carries real safety risk, management comes first, training second.

## Safety First

While training is underway, prevent access to situations where chasing is possible: secure fencing, leashes in unfenced areas, and supervision near roads. This isn't a shortcut around training — it's a non-negotiable safety layer while the underlying behavior is being addressed.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Build engagement with you around lower-intensity triggers first.** Practice name response and "leave it" around slower-moving distractions before tackling fast-moving ones like bikes or cars.
2. **Use distance and counter-conditioning.** Start far enough from the road or bike path that your dog notices the trigger without fully fixating, and reward calm attention toward you instead.
3. **Teach a strong "leave it" and recall.** These are your two most important safety tools — see our guides on [leave it and drop it](../teach-leave-it-and-drop-it/) and [recall training](../teach-dog-to-come-when-called/) to build them systematically.
4. **Gradually decrease distance to the trigger.** Only move closer once your dog stays engaged with you at the current distance across multiple sessions.
5. **Practice on leash exclusively until reliability is proven.** This isn't a behavior to test off-leash — the consequences of a failed attempt are too high.

## Common Mistakes

- Allowing off-leash access before the behavior is reliably under control, given the real safety risk involved.
- Only practicing in one location, then assuming it generalizes to every street or path.
- Punishing the chase instinct itself rather than building a stronger, well-reinforced alternative response.

## When to Get Structured Help

Because of the genuine danger involved — to your dog, drivers, cyclists, or joggers — a strong prey-drive-related chasing habit is worth addressing with a structured, professional training program rather than piecing together a plan from general tips alone.
