Barking at strangers is usually rooted in one of three things: fear, territorial instinct, or simple overexcitement. The fix depends on which one you're dealing with — but the training approach shares a common foundation.

## Identify the Trigger First

Watch your dog's body language. A tucked tail and backward lean signals fear. A forward, stiff posture near the door signals territorial guarding. A wiggly body with barking during play-like movement usually means excitement. This matters because fear-based barking should never be punished — it tends to make fear (and the barking) worse.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Manage the environment first.** Block visual access to the street through a window film or furniture placement if door/window barking is the issue. Less rehearsal of the behavior means faster progress.
2. **Desensitize at a distance.** Have a helper (or use a busy sidewalk) so your dog notices a stranger from far enough away that they stay under threshold — calm enough to take treats.
3. **Pair stranger-sighting with rewards.** Every time your dog notices a person without barking, mark it ("yes!") and reward. This builds a positive association: stranger = good things happen.
4. **Gradually decrease distance.** Over many short sessions, work closer as your dog stays calm, always moving at their pace, not a fixed schedule.
5. **Teach an alternative behavior.** A solid "go to mat" or "sit-stay" gives your dog a job to do at the door instead of rushing and barking.

## Common Mistakes

- Yelling "quiet," which often sounds like you're barking along and can increase arousal.
- Flooding: exposing your dog to too many strangers too fast, which sets training back.
- Only training in one location — generalize across the yard, walks, and the front door.

## Realistic Expectations

Reactive barking usually improves over weeks, not days, especially if it's fear-based. If progress stalls or the barking escalates into lunging, a structured, professional training program is worth following closely rather than guessing at fixes.
