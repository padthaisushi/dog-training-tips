A common myth is that formal training should wait until 6 months old. In reality, informal training can — and should — start the day you bring your puppy home. The "best age" depends more on what you're teaching than a single magic number.

## A Practical Age-by-Age Guide

- **8–12 weeks:** Focus on name recognition, hand-target, sit, and building a positive association with a crate. Keep sessions to 2–3 minutes, several times a day.
- **3–4 months:** Add "come," loose-leash walking basics, and impulse control games. This is also a key window for structured socialization.
- **4–6 months (teething):** Redirect chewing energy into training games; continue reinforcing earlier cues consistently as distractions increase.
- **6 months+:** Layer in duration and distraction — holding a "stay" longer, practicing recall around other dogs, and generalizing commands to new environments.

## Step-by-Step Starting Plan

1. **Start with short, frequent sessions.** 2–5 minutes, multiple times a day beats one long weekly session, especially for young puppies with short attention spans.
2. **Use rewards your dog actually wants.** Food, play, or praise — whichever motivates your individual dog most reliably.
3. **Train one behavior at a time.** Get a solid "sit" before layering in "stay," rather than teaching five commands loosely at once.
4. **Practice in different locations early.** A "sit" learned only in the kitchen doesn't reliably transfer to the backyard or a walk without separate practice.
5. **Adjust expectations to age, not ambition.** A 10-week-old puppy has a shorter attention span than a 6-month-old — that's normal, not a training failure.

## Common Mistakes

- Waiting for a "formal class age" before doing any training at all.
- Long, frustrating sessions that build negative associations with training itself.
- Skipping foundational cues to jump to advanced tricks.

## Adult Dogs Can Start Any Time

If you've adopted an older dog with no training history, the same principles apply — age is not a barrier to learning. A structured, step-by-step training program can help you build a plan appropriate to your dog's actual starting point rather than their age alone.
