Jumping is almost always an excitement and attention-seeking behavior — and it keeps happening because it keeps getting attention, even negative attention like pushing the dog off or saying "down." Fixing it means being consistent about what actually earns attention.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Remove the reward for jumping.** Turn away, fold your arms, and avoid eye contact the moment paws leave the ground. No pushing, no talking — jumping simply gets nothing.
2. **Reward four paws on the floor.** The instant your dog's feet are back down (or better, before they even jump), mark and reward calmly.
3. **Teach an incompatible behavior.** A solid "sit" for greetings gives your dog something to do instead of jumping — dogs can't sit and jump at the same time.
4. **Practice with easy scenarios first.** Have a calm family member greet your dog at low excitement before working up to the front door or excited guests.
5. **Get everyone on the same page.** Jumping only fades if all household members and regular visitors respond the same way, every time.

## Common Mistakes

- Allowing jumping when you're in casual clothes but correcting it when dressed up — dogs don't understand the distinction.
- Kneeing or pushing the dog away, which many dogs interpret as play and find rewarding.
- Only practicing at calm moments and never at the actual trigger point: the front door.

## Realistic Expectations

Consistent management (like a leash or gate at greetings) paired with reward-based training typically shows real improvement within a few weeks. For dogs with a strong, long-standing jumping habit, a structured obedience program can help build a solid "sit-to-greet" routine faster than piecemeal tips.
