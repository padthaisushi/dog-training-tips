Howling when alone can stem from a few different causes: separation-related distress, boredom, or a response to outside triggers (sirens, other dogs). The right fix depends on which one is driving it.

## Figure Out the Trigger

Set up a camera for a short absence. If howling starts within minutes of you leaving and is paired with pacing or destruction near exits, separation-related distress is likely. If it's intermittent and seems tied to outside sounds, it may be more of a response behavior than true anxiety. If it happens after long stretches of inactivity, boredom is a strong candidate.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **For separation-related howling**, use gradual desensitization: practice short, low-stress departures, extending duration slowly, and avoid emotional goodbyes or greetings that spike arousal around your leaving.
2. **For sound-triggered howling**, reduce exposure to the trigger where possible (white noise, closing blinds) and use counter-conditioning — pairing the trigger sound with something positive when you're home to practice.
3. **For boredom-driven howling**, increase exercise and mental enrichment before you leave — a long-lasting food puzzle at departure time can occupy the highest-risk early minutes.
4. **Avoid punishing the howling itself.** Since you're not present when it happens, delayed punishment doesn't connect to the behavior and can add general anxiety instead of reducing it.
5. **Build a consistent pre-departure routine** that doesn't telegraph "big deal, I'm leaving" — practice picking up keys and putting on shoes without leaving, so those cues stop predicting distress.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming all howling is separation anxiety without checking for other triggers first.
- Making departures and returns emotionally charged, which raises overall arousal around your absence.
- Only addressing the symptom (howling) without changing the underlying driver (boredom, anxiety, or trigger exposure).

## When to Get Structured Help

If howling is intense, escalating, or paired with other signs of distress (destruction, accidents only when alone, drooling), it's likely separation anxiety rather than a minor habit. A structured training program with a proper desensitization schedule can help address the root cause rather than just the noise.
