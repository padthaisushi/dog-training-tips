Crate training done right gives your puppy a safe den of their own — and gives you a reliable tool for house training, travel, and downtime. Done wrong, it creates anxiety. Here's the method that works.

## The Core Principle

The crate must always mean something good. Never use it as a punishment, and never rush the process. A puppy that's forced into a crate too fast will associate it with stress instead of safety.

## Step-by-Step Method

1. **Introduce it open and inviting.** Leave the crate door open with soft bedding and let your puppy explore on their own terms. Toss treats inside throughout the day.
2. **Feed meals in the crate.** Start with the bowl near the entrance, then gradually move it further back over several days.
3. **Add short, closed-door sessions.** Once your puppy eats calmly inside, close the door for 30–60 seconds while you sit nearby, then release before whining starts.
4. **Extend duration gradually.** Build up crate time in small increments over 1–2 weeks, always releasing during calm moments — not during barking or whining.
5. **Use it for sleep.** Once your puppy is comfortable, the crate becomes the overnight sleeping spot, ideally in or near your bedroom initially.

## Common Mistakes

- Going from "never crated" to "crated for hours" in one jump.
- Letting the puppy out while whining, which teaches whining works.
- Placing the crate somewhere isolated instead of near family activity at first.

## A Realistic Timeline

Most puppies can tolerate a few hours calmly within 2–3 weeks of consistent practice. Full-day comfort (for work schedules) usually takes longer and should be paired with a mid-day break plan. If your puppy is still highly distressed after a few weeks, a structured training program can help troubleshoot what's going wrong.
