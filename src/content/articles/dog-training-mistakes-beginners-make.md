Most training struggles aren't about a "stubborn" or "untrainable" dog — they trace back to a handful of common, fixable mistakes. Here are the ones that show up most often.

## 1. Poor Timing

Rewards (or corrections) need to land within about 1–2 seconds of the behavior to connect clearly in your dog's mind. Praising a few seconds late often ends up reinforcing whatever your dog is doing at that moment instead of the behavior you meant to reward.

**Fix:** Use a marker word ("yes!") or clicker at the exact moment of the correct behavior, then follow with the reward — the marker bridges the timing gap.

## 2. Inconsistency Across the Household

If one person allows jumping on the couch and another doesn't, or a cue word changes between "down" and "lay down," your dog gets mixed signals that slow every other lesson down.

**Fix:** Agree on cue words and house rules as a household before training begins, and write them down if needed.

## 3. Training Sessions That Are Too Long

Long sessions lead to frustration for both ends of the leash, especially with puppies or easily distracted dogs.

**Fix:** Keep sessions to 5–10 minutes, and end on a successful repetition rather than pushing until your dog disengages.

## 4. Skipping the Basics Under Distraction

A "sit" that only works in a quiet kitchen isn't actually reliable yet — it just hasn't been tested.

**Fix:** Practice known cues in gradually more distracting environments before expecting reliability in the real world (parks, other dogs, guests).

## 5. Punishing Fear-Based Behavior

Growling, barking, or avoidance rooted in fear or anxiety tends to get worse with punishment, not better — it just teaches the dog to suppress the warning signal.

**Fix:** Address the underlying emotional response with desensitization and counter-conditioning rather than suppressing the visible symptom.

## 6. Expecting Instant Results

Behavior change takes repetition — often weeks, not days — especially for deeply practiced habits like pulling or jumping.

**Fix:** Track small wins over a few weeks instead of judging progress session to session. If you're consistently making these fixes and still stuck, a structured, step-by-step training program can help identify what's actually going wrong.
