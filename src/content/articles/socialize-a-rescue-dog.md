Socializing a rescue dog is different from puppy socialization. You're often working with an unknown history, possible past trauma, and an adult nervous system that's already formed strong associations. The good news: adult dogs can absolutely learn new, positive associations — it just takes a slower, more deliberate approach.

## Start With Decompression

Many shelters and rescue groups recommend a "3-3-3" adjustment window (roughly 3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to settle into routine, 3 months to fully bond). Resist the urge to socialize heavily in the first days — let your dog settle into your home and routine first.

## Step-by-Step Approach

1. **Let your dog observe from a distance first.** Whether it's new people or other dogs, distance is your best tool. A dog that's calm from 30 feet away but reactive at 5 feet needs more distance, not more exposure.
2. **Pair new experiences with something good.** High-value treats delivered calmly during exposure to new people, sounds, or dogs build positive associations over repetition.
3. **Let your dog choose to approach.** Never force greetings. A rescue dog approaching on their own terms builds confidence; being pulled toward something scary erodes trust.
4. **Keep early sessions short.** Five calm minutes beats thirty overwhelming ones. End sessions before your dog shows stress signals (yawning, lip licking, turning away).
5. **Generalize gradually.** Once your dog is comfortable in one low-key setting, slowly introduce new locations, following the same distance-and-reward principle each time.

## Common Mistakes

- Hosting a "welcome home" gathering with lots of new people in the first week.
- Forcing interactions to "get it over with," which usually backfires.
- Comparing timelines to other dogs — every rescue's socialization pace is different.

## When Progress Stalls

If your dog shows ongoing fear, avoidance, or defensive behavior after a few months of patient work, a structured training program built for rescue and reactive dogs can provide a clearer, paced roadmap than general advice alone.
