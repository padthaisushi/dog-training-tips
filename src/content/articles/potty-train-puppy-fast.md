There's no shortcut that skips repetition, but you can potty train significantly faster by removing guesswork. Speed comes from tight supervision and a predictable schedule, not from any single trick.

## Step-by-Step Method

1. **Set a schedule based on age.** As a rule of thumb, puppies can hold their bladder roughly one hour per month of age, plus one. Take them out at least that often, plus after every meal, nap, and play session.
2. **Pick one designated spot.** Always take your puppy to the same outdoor spot on leash — the scent helps trigger the behavior faster than free-roaming in the yard.
3. **Reward immediately, outside.** Praise and treat within seconds of your puppy finishing, while still outside. Waiting until you're back inside teaches the wrong association.
4. **Supervise or confine — no free roaming.** When you can't watch directly, use a crate or playpen. Unsupervised access to the house is the #1 cause of slow progress.
5. **Interrupt, don't punish, accidents.** If you catch your puppy mid-accident, a calm "outside!" and quick trip out is enough. Punishment after the fact only teaches your puppy to hide from you, not to hold it.

## Common Mistakes

- Giving too much freedom too soon, before a consistent pattern is established.
- Cleaning accidents without an enzymatic cleaner, which leaves scent markers that invite repeat accidents.
- Inconsistent schedules on weekends, which slows the overall timeline.

## Realistic Timeline

Most puppies show solid progress within 4–6 weeks of a consistent schedule, with full reliability by 6 months for many breeds — though it can take longer for small breeds. If accidents continue past that with no improvement, a structured house-training program can help pinpoint what's going wrong in the routine.
