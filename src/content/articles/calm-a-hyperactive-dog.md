"Hyperactive" often means a dog whose exercise, mental stimulation, and structure needs aren't being fully met — though breed energy levels and individual temperament play a role too. The fix is rarely just "more exercise"; it's usually a combination of physical outlets, mental work, and deliberately trained calm behavior.

## Step-by-Step Plan

1. **Audit actual exercise needs.** High-energy breeds often need well over an hour of real activity daily — not just a short walk around the block. Match the amount and intensity to your specific dog.
2. **Add mental stimulation, not just physical.** Puzzle feeders, scent games, and short training sessions tire dogs out mentally, often more effectively than physical exercise alone.
3. **Train calm as its own skill.** Reward your dog for settling on a mat or bed during downtime — calm behavior that never gets reinforced tends to fade in favor of whatever does get attention.
4. **Build predictable structure.** A consistent daily routine (exercise, feeding, rest, training) reduces the unpredictability that can fuel excitability in some dogs.
5. **Avoid accidentally rewarding hyperactivity.** Giving attention, play, or access to something desired the moment your dog is amped up can reinforce that state — wait for a calmer moment before engaging.

## Common Mistakes

- Only addressing physical exercise while ignoring mental enrichment, which leaves a real need unmet.
- Engaging (even to scold) during peak excitement, which can function as reinforcement for some dogs.
- Expecting a naturally high-energy breed to become low-key without matching their actual activity requirements.

## When It's More Than Energy

If hyperactivity is paired with an inability to settle even after real exercise, or looks more like anxiety-driven restlessness, it may be worth treating as a distinct issue rather than simple excess energy. A structured training program focused on calm-behavior conditioning can help build the specific skill of settling down, which doesn't always develop on its own.
