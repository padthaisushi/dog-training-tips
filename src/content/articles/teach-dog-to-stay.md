A truly reliable "stay" isn't taught in one session — it's built by training three separate variables independently: how long your dog holds the position (duration), how far you move away (distance), and how much is happening around them (distraction). Mixing all three too soon is the most common reason "stay" falls apart in real situations.

## Step-by-Step Method

1. **Start with duration only, at zero distance.** Ask for a sit, say "stay," and reward after just 2–3 seconds while standing right next to your dog. Gradually extend the time before rewarding.
2. **Add distance only once duration is solid.** Keep the time short again, but take one step back before returning to reward. Increase distance gradually, resetting duration expectations lower each time you add distance.
3. **Add distraction last, at low duration and distance.** Introduce mild distractions (a dropped object, someone walking by) only once the basics are reliable, again lowering the other two variables temporarily.
4. **Always release with a clear word.** Use a consistent release cue ("okay," "free") so your dog learns "stay" means "until I say otherwise," not an indefinite guess.
5. **Reward generously for holding position, and return to reward — don't call your dog to you.** Walking back to reward keeps the stay intact; calling your dog toward you teaches them to anticipate breaking position.

## Common Mistakes

- Increasing duration, distance, and distraction all at once, which usually causes the stay to collapse.
- Rewarding after calling the dog toward you instead of returning to reward, which undermines the position.
- Practicing only in one quiet room and expecting reliability in a busy environment without separate distraction training.

## Realistic Expectations

Basic stay reliability at home often develops within 1–2 weeks; full reliability across distance and real-world distractions can take considerably longer. If you want stay trained as part of a full, structured obedience sequence rather than in isolation, a step-by-step training program can help build it alongside your other cues.
